Theoretical Physics Insights:

  • Mass-Dependent Jacobian: The conversion is scaled by $J = p/E$. Because $E = \sqrt{m_T^2 \cosh^2 y}$, heavier mass suppresses the Jacobian.
  • Mid-rapidity Dip: At $y = 0$, $J = p_T / \sqrt{p_T^2 + m^2}$. As $p_T \to 0$ or $m \to \infty$, $J \to 0$, creating a deep dip in $dN/d\eta$ at $\eta=0$.
  • Massless / High-pT Limit: As $p_T \gg m$, the particle becomes ultra-relativistic ($\beta \to 1$), the Jacobian factor $J \to 1$, and $\eta \approx y$.
