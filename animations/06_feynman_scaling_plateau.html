Longitudinal Scale-Invariance & Rapidity Plateau

Feynman scaling describes the scaling behavior of longitudinal momentum distributions at high energies. Toggle between Rapidity (y) and Feynman ($x_F$) plots. Notice how:

y = sinh⁻¹(x_F * √s / m_T)

As you raise the collision energy $\sqrt{s}$, the rapidity distribution extends outward, and a flat **rapidity plateau** develops at mid-rapidity. In contrast, on the Feynman $x_F$ basis, the distributions at different energies scale (superimpose) in the fragmentation regions ($|x_F| \sim 0.1 \to 0.8$), while the expanding rapidity plateau is compressed into a narrow peak at $x_F \approx 0$.
