Phase Transition Scenario
Current Phase: Hadron Gas (Low Density)
What you are seeing: In 1982, Leon Van Hove proposed that plotting average transverse momentum <p_T> (which acts as a proxy for the fireball's **temperature**) as a function of the charged particle multiplicity density dN_ch/dη (which acts as a proxy for **entropy density**) provides a clear signature of QGP formation:
1. Hadron Gas Phase (dN_ch/dη < 30): As energy increases, the hadronic gas absorbs energy and heats up rapidly. The pressure rises, causing <p_T> to increase.
2. Mixed Phase / Phase Coexistence (30 < dN_ch/dη < 75): As the fireball reaches the critical temperature T_c ≈ 155 MeV, hadrons begin to melt into free quarks and gluons. Like boiling water, adding energy (which increases multiplicity/entropy) does not increase the temperature; the latent heat of deconfinement is absorbed. This produces the **characteristic flat plateau** in <p_T>.
3. QGP Phase (dN_ch/dη > 75): Once all hadrons have melted, adding more energy heats the pure Quark-Gluon Plasma, causing the temperature and <p_T> to rise again.
Compare: Toggle between **Crossover** (a smooth rise representing high-energy LHC collisions where there is no sharp phase boundary) and **No QGP** (temperature rises indefinitely, which would happen if confinement was never broken).
