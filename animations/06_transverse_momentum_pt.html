How pT is measured:  A solenoid field B runs along the beam (z-axis). Charged particles curve in the transverse (x–y) plane. The radius of curvature R = pT / (0.3·B) is measured by fitting the curved hit pattern in the TPC. High pT → large R → nearly straight track. Low pT → small R → tight spiral.  |  Why spectators have pT ≈ 0: they travel along the beam (θ ≈ 0°), receive no transverse kick, and fly straight into the Zero Degree Calorimeter (ZDC) — invisible to the barrel TPC.  |  pT is Lorentz-invariant under longitudinal boosts (changing reference frame along z) — which is why it is the preferred variable in heavy-ion physics.
